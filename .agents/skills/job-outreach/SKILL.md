# Job outreach

Help a hiring manager understand the user's relevance quickly. Use a conversational, specific voice with enough detail to support a next step.

## Establish the facts

Read the hiring post, role, or conversation and follow its application instructions. Research the actual company and opening when needed. Use the user's supplied resume, projects, and experience to identify the strongest relevant connection.

Reuse available context. Ask a focused question only when a missing fact materially changes the draft. Never invent experience, metrics, company problems, product use, completed projects, or actions already taken.

## Match the language

Use natural contractions and direct verbs: "I lead," "I've been building," "I work on." Brief fragments can sound natural when their meaning is clear. Let concrete responsibilities and work demonstrate competence.

Match the user's voice and the recipient's context. Do not mechanically copy another person's capitalization, jargon, employer, title, or achievements. Ordinary phrases such as "Would love to chat" are fine when they fit. Avoid generic praise, inflated claims, and long autobiographical introductions.

## Choose the form

Public hiring reply: keep it to a brief expression of relevant interest and, when intended, a move to DMs. The source example is "This looks like a perfect role. DMing!" Use comparable enthusiasm only when warranted. Do not claim a message has already been sent unless confirmed.

First DM: open with the specific hiring post or role. Establish current work or relevant project experience, give one or two details that connect it to the opening, then invite a conversation. A short paragraph plus a separate closing is often enough; do not force a sentence quota.

A reusable shape, with placeholders to replace:
"Hi [name], saw your post about [role]. I'm [current role/context], where I [relevant responsibility]. I've been [specific related work]. Would love to chat if you think there could be a fit."

Metrics and portfolio links are optional. Include them when verified and useful. The screenshot's DM demonstrates relevance without either.

Recruiting response: answer the recipient's question first. When asked for an email or availability, provide the user's confirmed details and a brief acknowledgment. Do not restart the pitch.

Cover letter: adapt the same concrete voice into the requested format. Explain why this opening fits, support that connection with relevant work, and close simply. This is an extension of the source principles, not a cover-letter format shown in the screenshots.

## Strengthen weak evidence

When useful, suggest a bounded work sample related to a researched need. A custom project is optional, not a prerequisite for every application. Distinguish a hypothesis from a known company problem and a prototype from deployed work.

## Deliver and check

Return the requested finished message. Identify unresolved facts separately; do not present placeholders as ready to send. Check factual support, relevance, natural phrasing, and the stated next step.

Keep the public comment and private DM distinct. Drafting does not authorize posting, liking, applying, or sending; use the authorization actually supplied by the user.
